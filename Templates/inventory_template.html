Forecast variables explained

A — On-hand stock
The current quantity in inventory when this forecast is calculated. Historical demand is not subtracted from it again.
Average demand
The mean of the saved demand history, expressed per month in the six-month section and per year in the annual section.
σ (sigma)
The population standard deviation of historical demand; it measures how much demand varies.
z — Service-level factor
The safety factor for the selected service level. This model uses a 95% target, so z = 1.65.
T — Review interval
Time between reviews: 6 months for six-month products and 12 months for annual products.
L — Lead time
Time from placing an order until receipt: 1 month for six-month products and 2 months for annual products.
SS — Safety stock
Extra stock for demand variation. Six-month formula: z × σ × √(T + L). Annual formula: z × σ.
S — Target stock
Desired stock after replenishment. Six-month formula: average demand × (T + L) + SS. Annual formula: average annual demand + SS.
Recommended order
Maximum of zero or the amount needed to reach the target: ceil(S − A). Estimated cost is this quantity × unit price.
